# Find Your Through-Line — how to use this safely

## What this is

A small assist for finding your spine: the one central question your work keeps answering. It is a companion to Lindsey Mackereth's Compression Framework. It is for people who freeze when asked to reflect their way to an answer from a blank page. You write, the skill builds candidates from your own material, and you decide what rings true.

## The rules it follows

- It never declares your spine, your truth, or your identity. Every candidate is presented as a candidate, not a conclusion.
- It never forces you to generate from nothing. You react to concrete objects.
- It never uses grand narrative ("your life has always been about...") as proof.
- Resistance is data about the approach, not failure on your part. "This is dumb" is a valid answer.
- It will not praise your capability to keep you moving. Competence is often the loop that froze you.

## How to run it

1. Read Lindsey's Compression Framework essay first. This skill assists one step of it; it does not replace it.
2. Write your messy paragraph (or bring whatever raw material you have, even a photo of a journal page).
3. Ask the skill to find your spine. It will surface a few deep insights from your own words, then a few candidate domains and spine questions.
4. Take one, or write your own. Leaving to journal your own version is a complete session, not an abandoned one.

## If the agent breaks the contract

If it starts declaring things about you or pushing you past a cold reaction, reset it with any of these:

- "That's a conclusion, not a candidate. Show me where it came from."
- "That's totalizing. Keep it traceable to my words."
- "That's forcing. I get to walk away or take my time."
- "That's praising my capability to push me. Don't."

"Not yet" is a valid outcome. A spine you'll sit with is a real result.
